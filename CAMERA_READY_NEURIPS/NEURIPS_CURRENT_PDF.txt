Abstract
Wrong-level spine surgery remains a persistent never-event, with the Lumbosacral1
Transitional Vertebra (LSTV , 5–35% prevalence) as its largest anatomic driver. We2
audit TotalSegmentator—the most widely deployed CT segmentation system and3
default back-end of 3D Slicer—and show that on lumbarization cases its output4
is anatomically indistinguishable from a wrong-level surgical plan: lacking an L65
class, it shifts lumbar labels caudally by one level, with junction-DSC dropping6
29 points on Any-LSTV . No existing CT benchmark surfaces this—not TotalSeg-7
mentator’s own evaluation, not Li et al. [2025] (the per-class DSC frontier on8
the matched COLONOG split), and not VERIDAH (vertebra-labelingSOTA on9
a private CT cohort, operating on pre-localized crops with no pelvis). We con-10
tribute: (i) an LSTV-stratified evaluation protocol (per-class DSC, junction-DSC11
over a 40 mm L5/S1 window, per-class voxel confusion at the junction) translating12
the level-shift mechanism into quantifiable wrong-level surgical risk; (ii) CT-13
SPINOPELVIC1K—1,153 CT volumes across 802 patients with unified spinopelvic14
masks, 33 LSTV-positive cases across a 6-way phenotype taxonomy, and radiolo-15
gist Castellvi typing (I–IV) on all 33; (iii) the first publicly-released merge-based16
LSTV-handling dual-spinopelvic CT segmenter, exceeding TotalSegmentator on17
sacrum (TotalSegmentator=0.817 zero-shot) and uniquely delivering both spine and18
pelvis in a single forward pass with released weights; (iv) an empirical isolation19
showing VERIDAH’s training-side L5/L6 merge eliminates the collision driving20
TotalSegmentator’s level shift but exposes a residual L4/last_lumbar collision on21
count-style sacralization, motivating VERIDAH’s sequence predictor as the neces-22
sary disambiguation component. CTSPINOPELVIC1K, the protocol, and 5-fold23
checkpoints are released publicly.24
1 Introduction25
Wrong-level spine surgery is a persistent never-event in operative spine care [Konin and Walz, 2010].26
Despite intraoperative imaging and surgical-pause checklists, the most common cause—failure to27
correctly count vertebral levels in the presence of an LSTV—continues to account for the majority of28
reported wrong-level lumbar incidents [Carrino et al., 2011]. The mechanism is well documented: a29
surgeon counts lumbar bodies upward from the sacrum, finds five (because lumbarization presents30
a sixth lumbar body that has not been recognized), and labels the bottom body as L5 when it is31
anatomically L6. Surgery proceeds one level caudal to the intended target.32
Modern radiology workflows increasingly route automated segmentation as an upstream substrate33
for clinical decision-making. The most widely deployed CT segmentation system in this role is34
TotalSegmentator [Wasserthal et al., 2023]: an nnU-Net trained on 1,204 routine CTs to delineate 10435

The clinical claim.On lumbarized spines, TotalSegmentator produces an output pattern that44
is, when consumed by surgical planning pipelines or by surgeons confirming operative levels,45
indistinguishable from a wrong-level surgical plan. Its label vocabulary contains L1–L5 but no L646
class. On a lumbarized spine (six lumbar bodies plus sacrum), the model distributes its five lumbar47
labels across the six bodies by shifting caudally by one anatomic level: the body that is anatomically48
L6 receives the L5 label, the body that is anatomically L5 receives the L4 label, and so on. A surgeon49
targeting the disc between predicted-L4 and predicted-L5 lands on the anatomical L5–L6 disc, one50
level caudal to the intended L4–L5 target. TotalSegmentator reproduces the canonical LSTV-induced51
miscount automatically and silently.52
The architectural alternative: VERIDAH.Recent work [Möller et al., 2026] addresses transi-53
tional vertebra labeling via a two-stage architecture: a 3D DenseNet169 classifier on per-vertebra54
crops (four heads: label, region, transition, visibility), followed by a constrained sequence predictor55
that imposes anatomical-count constraints (4–6 lumbar bodies; the predicted sequence may end in56
two consecutive L5 labels, the second interpreted as L6). The classifier is trained with L5/L6 (and57
T12/T13) labels merged for class-imbalance reasons; disambiguation is deferred to the sequence58
predictor. The architectural commitment is empirically validated (97.22% CT subject-level agree-59
ment) but has never been tested against direct semantic segmentation, and it is unknown whether a60
sufficiently expressive voxel-wise network trained at full resolution with the same merge requires the61
sequence predictor at all. We supply the missing test.62
The evaluation gap.Neither TotalSegmentator, VerSe [Sekuboyina et al., 2021], CTSpine1K [Deng63
et al., 2021], nor CTPelvic1K [Liu et al., 2021] report LSTV-stratified performance. Earlier CT64
labelers explicitly excluded the failure mode [Sekuboyina et al., 2020]. VERIDAH reports anomaly65
performance on a private cohort without isolating semantic-segmentation contribution. The result is a66
measurement vacuum: a production model emits a confident, locally well-formed, caudally-shifted67
prediction on a 5–35%-prevalence congenital variant with no error signal at evaluation time.68
Closing the gap requires hardware.Fitting both lumbar spine and pelvis at69
0.75×0.75×0.80 mm spacing and 256×320×320 patch consumes ∼100 GB GPU memory70
under nnUNetResEncUNetPlans_100G, available on H200 (140 GB HBM3e) but not on the71
L40S/A100/H100 GPUs that bracket TotalSegmentator’s training configuration. Neither VERIDAH72
nor SPINEPS train at this combined spinopelvic resolution.73

2 Related Work100
Wrong-level spine surgery and LSTV .[Konin and Walz, 2010, Carrino et al., 2011, Sekharappa101
et al., 2014] document LSTV-induced miscounting (5–35% prevalence) without addressing auto-102
mated segmentation as a miscount-propagation pathway. Kwak et al. [2025] detect LSTV on plain103
radiographs (binary; different modality).104
Production CT spine and pelvic segmentation.TotalSegmentator [Wasserthal et al., 2023], an105
nnU-Net trained on 1,204 CTs and the default 3D Slicer back-end, has no L6 class and no LSTV106
stratification; we audit it as the production system downstream pipelines actually consume, not as a107
per-class DSC frontier. Li et al. [2025] hold the published per-class DSC frontier on a COLONOG108
split (731 scans; 440/146/145) but release neither weights nor code (paper-only) and do not stratify109
by LSTV . Liu et al. [2025] release FracSegNet (PENGWIN+CTPelvic1K), a pelvic-only cascaded110
3D nnU-Net providing sacrum and L/R hipbone; their qualitative note of sacralization degradation111
(their §4.3) corroborates the LSTV gap our benchmark surfaces. Table 5 compares all baselines.112
Vertebra-labeling SOTA: VERIDAH.Möller et al. [2026] address transitional-vertebralabeling113
(distinct from segmentation): a four-head 3D DenseNet169 classifier on per-vertebra crops plus114
a constrained sequence predictor enforcing 4–6 lumbar bodies (allowing terminal double-L5 to115
encode L6). On a private CT cohort of 1,536 volumes VERIDAH reports 99.18% subject-level116
perfect labeling versus prior CT SOTA [Meng et al., 2023] 77.81%. Three properties matter for us:117
VERIDAH (i) requires upstream centroids (VerSe-derived models or SPINEPS [Möller et al., 2024],118
itself MRI-only); (ii) excludes pelvis; (iii) validates on a private CT cohort. Their ablation (Table 5)119
shows the sequence predictor adds substantial value, but the comparison against direct semantic120
segmentation on a public LSTV-stratified split is missing—the gap our CTSPINOPELVIC1K-trained121
baseline (§7) fills.122
Benchmarks and evaluation rigor.VerSe [Sekuboyina et al., 2021] (460 CT volumes) is the only123
public CT benchmark whose label vocabulary includes L6 (label 25) but it is unstratified by LSTV124
and lacks pelvic GT; earlier CT labelers explicitly excluded the failure mode [Sekuboyina et al.,125
2020]. Stratified safety-critical reporting [Maier-Hein et al., 2024] motivates §6.126

The level-shift mechanism.The asymmetry between lumbarization (severe, caudally progressive)139
and count-style sacralization (uniform collapse) follows from a single fact: TotalSegmentator’s output140
head has no L6 class. For 6-vertebra anatomies the model compresses 6 anatomic units into 5 labels by141
shifting label assignment caudally; the most rostral body remains L1 (escape into thoracic vertebrae),142
but every successive body is misaligned, with the final body labeled L5 when it is anatomically L6.143
For 4-vertebra anatomies the model emits standard L1–L5, overshooting the GT lumbar count and144
producing a +1-level offset uniformly. The level-shift mechanism is the segmentation analog of the145
surgical miscount [Konin and Walz, 2010].146

Morphology-stratified failure interpretation.The cross-tabulation (Table 4) surfaces two findings205
with direct bearing on model behavior. (1)All 9 count-style sacralization cases are Castellvi IIIb—206

segmenter still emits last_lumbar for those voxels. Direct semantic segmentation has not learned the210

6 Evaluation Protocol218
We define four metrics as the minimum required to attribute LSTV failure modes to wrong-219
level surgical risk [Maier-Hein et al., 2024]: (1) per-class DSC stratified by 6-way LSTV220
subgroup, exposing class-level degradation; (2) junction-DSC over a 40 mm L5/S1 axial221
window, the clinical window for surgical planning; (3) per-class voxel confusion at the222
L5/S1 junction, which exposes structured failure modes (the level shift) that DSC alone223
cannot characterize; and (4) for the merged-label baseline, three focused per-epoch con-224
fusion blocks anchored on the L4/last_lumbar boundary that drives the residual collision225
(last_lumbar_GT_on_lumb, L4_GT_on_sacr_count, pred_last_lumbar_on_sacr_count)226
plus a specificity number (last_lumbar_specificity_on_sacr_count).227

Interpretation.The model has learned the spinopelvic spatial relationship on normal anatomy and286
recovers the merged last_lumbar class on lumbarization. On count-style sacralization, however, the287
positional prior dominates: the bottom-most lumbar visible in a given patch is treated as last_lumbar288
regardless of the global vertebral count. The disambiguating signal (vertebra count from a known289
anchor) is non-local; a 256×320×320 patch does not reliably contain both the caudal lumbosacral290
and rostral cervico-thoracic boundaries needed to count. This is a finding rather than a defect291
of the baseline—it executes the cleanest test of VERIDAH’s training-side merge in the semantic-292
segmentation setting and confirms the architectural hypothesis underlying its two-stage design: the293
merge addresses class imbalance, but distinguishing the last-lumbar voxels of a 4-vertebra spine294
from those of a 5-vertebra spine must be deferred to a downstream component with access to global295
anatomical structure.296

9 Discussion and Future Work297
Generalization and hardware.Any spine segmentation model lacking an L6 class will exhibit the298
level-shift on lumbarization; the LSTV gap is the symptom, not a model-specific bug. Our 100 GB299
patch budget requires H200-class hardware; smaller budgets yield weaker gap closure and therefore300
residual wrong-level risk downstream.301
Future work. (1) VerSe expansion via sacrum pseudolabeling.VerSe [Sekuboyina et al., 2021]302
contains 460 CT volumes labeling L6 (label 25) but no pelvis; our segmenter’s sacral DSC (0.969±303
0.002, 5-fold) supports sacrum pseudolabeling on VerSe for a 1,613-volume spinopelvic cohort.304
(2) Two-stage integration with VERIDAH.Our segmenter can replace VERIDAH’s VerSe-derived305
CT upstream, supplying sacrum and per-vertebra centroids in a single forward pass.306
Limitations.COLONOG is a US screening cohort (ages 45–75); generalization to emer-307
gency/pediatric populations is not evaluated. LSTV labels are inherited from CTPelvic1K and308
CTSpine1K heuristics; Castellvi annotation covers all 33 LSTV cases but does not screen thenormal309
cohort for Type I, and the source-dataset construction biases against morphologically-mild Type I310
presentations (§5). The held-out test split contains 7 of the 33 LSTV cases (3 lumb, 1 sacr_count, 2311
sacralization-morph, 1 ambiguous, 0 semi-sacralization); fold-level evaluation pools across the same312
174-case test set, yielding 15 case-fold pairs for lumbarization and 5 for sacr_count in voxel-confusion313
analysis but only the test-split n for case-level Dice (voxels within a case are highly correlated, so314
voxel counts in §8.1 represent dependent observations rather than independent samples). We do not315
run a same-compute unmerged 9-class baseline as a controlled ablation of the merge contribution;316
the merge’s role is inferred from comparison to TS (which has no L6 class) rather than a same-data317
ablation. TS is evaluated zero-shot, the correct comparison for using TS as a production system but318
undersells what it could achieve if retrained with an L6 class.319
Implications.A production CT segmentation system that emits a confident, locally well-formed,320
caudally-shifted prediction on a 5–35%-prevalence congenital variant produces no error signal at321
inference time and, when consumed downstream, reproduces the canonical mechanism of wrong-322
level spine surgery. Any future spine segmentation model claiming readiness for surgical-planning323
integration should report per-subgroup DSC and per-class confusion at the L5/S1 junction on a324
stratified LSTV cohort.325
