Keywords: CT segmentation, lumbosacral transitional vertebra, LSTV, wrong-level surgery, spine surgical planning, nnU-
Net, TotalSegmentator, transitional vertebra labeling, vertebra counting, spinopelvic anatomy, Castellvi classification, sacrum

TL;DR: TotalSegmentator silently mislabels LSTV cases one level off—reproducing the wrong-level surgery error. We release
CTSpinoPelvic1K (LSTV-stratified, Castellvi-typed) and the first openly-released dual spinopelvic CT segmenter.
Abstract:
Wrong-level spine surgery remains a persistent never-event, with the Lumbosacral Transitional Vertebra (LSTV, 5–35%
prevalence) as its largest anatomic driver. We audit TotalSegmentator — the most widely deployed CT segmentation system
and default back-end of 3D Slicer — and show that on lumbarization cases its output is anatomically indistinguishable from a
wrong-level surgical plan: lacking an L6 class, it shifts lumbar labels caudally by one level, with junction-DSC dropping 29
points on Any-LSTV. No existing CT benchmark surfaces this — not TotalSegmentator's own evaluation, not Li et al. (2025) (the
per-class DSC frontier on the matched COLONOG split), and not VERIDAH (vertebra-labeling SOTA on a private CT cohort,
operating on pre-localized crops with no pelvis). We contribute: (i) an LSTV-stratified evaluation protocol (per-class DSC,
junction-DSC over a 40 mm L5/S1 window, per-class voxel confusion at the junction) translating the level-shift mechanism into
quantifiable wrong-level surgical risk; (ii) CTSpinoPelvic1K — 1,153 CT volumes across 802 patients with unified spinopelvic
masks, 33 LSTV-positive cases across a 6-way phenotype taxonomy, and radiologist Castellvi typing (I–IV) on all 33; (iii) the first
publicly-released merge-based LSTV-handling dual-spinopelvic CT segmenter, exceeding TotalSegmentator on sacrum
(TS=0.817 zero-shot) and uniquely delivering both spine and pelvis in a single forward pass with released weights; (iv) an
empirical isolation showing VERIDAH's training-side L5/L6 merge eliminates the collision driving TotalSegmentator's level shift
but exposes a residual L4/last_lumbar collision on count-style sacralization, motivating VERIDAH's sequence predictor as the
necessary disambiguation component. CTSpinoPelvic1K, the protocol, and 5-fold checkpoints are released publicly.

added.
(e) I read the response. The paper has two layers. A segmenter with no L6 class shifts lumbar labels one level caudally,
and rerunning it on released volumes reproduces this without any contested read; the unmerged same-data control I

holding its rating on the annotation layer. Fbco moved from 3 to 4 and jbUM held at 5, neither posting.

Final ratings are 3, 4 and 5, ranking 4/10 in my pool, with the highest score from the most confident reviewer.

Strengths:
The problem is clinically important and well motivated: wrong-level spine surgery is a persistent never-event, LSTV is

Weaknesses:
1. The entire LSTV evaluation rests on only 33 LSTV-positive cases, several subtypes have just a handful (e.g., count-

numbers characterize the conspicuous regime rather than the full LSTV population. Therefore, the authors
should report uncertainty for the small subgroups, expand the rare phenotypes (especially Type I), and restrict
the quantitative claims to the conspicuous regime that the 33 cases actually cover.
2. The new LSTV and Castellvi annotation layers that give the benchmark its value are verified only by two resident
radiologists by consensus, with no inter-rater scoring and no senior adjudication (which the paper
acknowledges), so confidence in the ground truth and in every downstream LSTV-stratified result is limited.
Therefore, the authors should report an inter-rater agreement metric (e.g., Cohen's kappa) and add senior-
radiologist adjudication on at least a representative subset of the 33 cases.
3. The advantage attributed to the L5/L6 merge is inferred from a zero-shot comparison to TotalSegmentator, which
structurally lacks an L6 class, rather than from a same-data unmerged baseline, so the benefit of the merge
cannot be separated from that of the new data and the added L6 class. Therefore, the authors should add a
same-data control, namely an unmerged baseline trained on CTSpinoPelvic1K and ideally a TotalSegmentator-
style model retrained with an L6 class, to isolate the merge's contribution.
4. The residual L4/last_lumbar collision that motivates the fourth contribution shows high cross-fold variability
(Table 6 reports an L4 Dice of 0.187 with a standard deviation of 0.337, driven by one outlier fold) and pools
correlated voxels across only a handful of sacralization cases, so its statistical basis is weak. Therefore, the
authors should report case-level rather than voxel-level uncertainty and confirm the finding on substantially
more sacralization cases.
