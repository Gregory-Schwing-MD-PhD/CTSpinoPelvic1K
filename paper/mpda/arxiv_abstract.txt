Lumbar levels cannot be numbered reliably when a lumbosacral transitional vertebra is
present. Sacralisation and lumbarisation leave different traces, but those traces are not
decisive in every case, and the count that would arbitrate is taken downward from C2 - the one
landmark present in every spine and never duplicated. An abdominal field of view contains no
such landmark, so the name rests on morphology exactly where morphology is least certain.
Existing public collections either omit the pelvis or omit the classes a transitional vertebra
requires: the largest whole-body collection provides no sixth lumbar vertebra and no class for
a rib arising from a lumbar level, and VerSe excludes vertebrae fused to the sacrum by design.

CTSpinoPelvic1K provides 802 abdominal CT records carrying radiologist-sourced spine and
pelvic annotation on one coordinate frame, with per-level ribs, femora and surgical
instrumentation. Classes exist so that a variant need not be forced into an ordinary level: a
sixth lumbar vertebra, a thirteenth thoracic vertebra, the first sacral segment held separate
from the sacrum, and left and right lumbar ribs. What the source collections never published,
and what this release adds, is the mapping from each annotation to the CT series it was drawn
on. Validation is threefold: per-case geometric invariants (802/802 pass), rib-vertebra
incidence across 5,749 evaluable ribs (2 residual offsets, 0.035%), and agreement of derived
spinopelvic measures with published reference values.

Limitations: thoracic ground truth is field-of-view limited; postural angles are supine; the
splits are cross-validation folds with no held-out test set; the rib layer is a reviewed
pseudolabel whose review was triaged automatically rather than exhaustively; and the Castellvi
grades are one reader's.
